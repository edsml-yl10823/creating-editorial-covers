# Creating Editorial Covers

Make the article's point visible as a concrete event that reads at thumbnail size. Read the [approved series references](references/series-cover-reference.md) and inspect the examples that best fit the assignment.

## Decide what to deliver

- For a standard cover request, generate five candidates in the same visual series. Give each a distinct event or visual metaphor. Keep the drawing style and the article's palette direction consistent across the set.
- For a trial, a follow-up article in a trial sequence, or a revision of one cover, start with one strong image. An explicit image count, selected concept, or request for text-only ideas takes precedence.
- Proceed directly to generation when the user asks for images and the brief is sufficient. Resolve a selection by letter, number, or name against the most recent set.

## Find the scene

1. Distill the headline and article into one specific claim or tension. Use details from the article to decide what the picture should show.
2. Describe the scene in one sentence. Identify who or what acts, in which setting, and what unexpected result follows. Make one action carry the claim. For multiple candidates, vary the action, setting, or metaphor as well as the composition.
3. Test the idea with any headline and brand names covered. The viewer should still be able to tell what is happening. If the scene only says “fast”, “strong”, or “cheap” and could fit an unrelated article, rework the cause and effect around a concrete detail from the piece.

## Cast the brands and choose colors

- When product identity matters, inspect the user's assets or current official product visuals. Pick an identifiable icon, mascot, or product shape for each key brand and give it a job in the scene. Keep its recognizable silhouette, colors, and distinguishing details when illustrating it as a character or prop.
- Use a brief product or model label when the specific version needs identification. Keep the image readable through the action and brand graphics. If a current icon cannot be verified, use an accurate product cue or exact short name. Apply any supplied logo or campaign requirements.
- For editorial stories, start with a warm direction and adjust it to the subject. For partnerships and product-led covers, choose colors from the current campaign or lead brand assets. Preserve other key brands' colors on their cues and use supporting colors to clarify the scene. Shift the dominant color and accents across stories as needed.

## Illustrate, check, and save

1. Use a 2.25:1 horizontal canvas for WeChat covers and as the default delivery format. Carry the delivery aspect ratio and orientation through all candidates, revisions, and follow-ups. Change the format only when the user explicitly requests a different deliverable format. Treat platform mentions, hashtags, and preview labels in pasted material as article context. Use bold flat shapes, elastic proportions, selective linework, and room around the focal action. Let the story determine the motion, framing, and amount of detail. Keep the action and essential brand cues readable near the center.
2. Use ImageGen for raster illustrations. Give each prompt the article claim, the single event, the role of each reference and brand cue, the palette, and any exact text. Reference other publications for compositional ideas and draw an original scene with its own layout. Keep publication mastheads, watermarks, and decorative pseudo-data out of the image.
3. Check the actual pixel dimensions and final crop against the delivery aspect ratio and orientation. Review at full size and at roughly 400 px wide. Check that the event reads immediately, the required brand cues remain recognizable, and any exact text is accurate. For a set, compare the candidates together for a shared visual style and meaningfully different ideas. Correct format mismatches before delivery and revise weak images with a targeted change.
4. Save the outputs in the workspace using descriptive filenames. Report the saved paths and a short description of the visual directions. End the delivery by asking the user which candidate they choose by letter or name. If there is one image, ask whether they choose that image.
5. Treat an unambiguous user selection as approval to archive the chosen original in `assets/generated/` in the same turn. Add an entry for it in [the series reference notes](references/series-cover-reference.md) with the design choices future covers can reuse.
